The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) is a network of 66 radio antennas on the Chajnantor plateau in northern Chile, at an altitude of about 5,000 metres. It observes light at millimetre and submillimetre wavelengths, where the cool dust in planet-forming discs glows brightly and where molecules in the disc's gas emit at very specific frequencies.

Because the antennas work together as an interferometer, ALMA can see fine detail, comparable to a single telescope as wide as the whole array (up to around 16 kilometres across). Since ALMA's 2014 image of the young star HL Tau, observations have shown that many young discs contain rings, gaps and other structures. Forming planets are one of the possible explanations, which is why these structures are a main target of my research.

More on the observatory: [almaobservatory.org](https://www.almaobservatory.org/).
