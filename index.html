Dr. Jochen Stadler

Postdoctoral Fellow · European Southern Observatory (ESO), Garching bei München

Hi therer! I’m Joe, a postdoctoral fellow at the European Southern Observatory (ESO) in Garching near Munich. In my research, I study how planets form. With the Atacama Large Milimeter Array (ALMA) I observe the gas and dust in the disks around newborn stars. In particular, I observe the motion of molecular gas to look for the subtle dynamcial signs that young embeded protoplanets imprint on their natal disks. I’m also interested in how the kinematics and the chemistry of these disks fit together.

I did my PhD at the Observatoire de la Côte d’Azur (OCA) in Nice with Dr. Myriam Benisty (2022–2025) in her ERC PROTOPLANETS research group, after studying physics at University of Heidelberg. I’m part of the exoALMA collaboration, where I co-led the kinematics analysis team, and of the CHEER ALMA Large Program on the chemistry of Herbig disks.

Beyond research, I give planetarium shows at the ESO Supernova and I’m part of Astronomers for Planet Earth. Before astronomy, there was gastronomy (just drop the G): I grew up in my family’s small inn and restaurant, Gasthof Lehner, and later trained at a 4-star hotel, so hospitality has been part of my life for many years. You can find my publications, my CV and my outreach activities on this site.
